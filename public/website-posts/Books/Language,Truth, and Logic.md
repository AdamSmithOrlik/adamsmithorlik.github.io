Language, Truth, and Logic is a short philosophy book by A.J. Ayer in which he advances the principle of verification as the criterion of meaning. Ayer argues that a statement is only literally significant or meaningful if it can in principle be empirically verified. This simple idea has immense philosophical implications, damning to the realm of metaphysics much of the history of philosophy. In this short book Ayer shows how the principle of verification can be used to solve or resolve many of the open problems of philosophy. Although many of the ideas have since been refuted or refined, the style of argumentation and the contrarian spirit of Ayer make this book an enjoyable read. For my part, this brand of empiricism, however dated, appeals to me as it reduces the complexity of the world to a simple logic of sense-contents and a scientific reframing of language wherein sentences serve as hypotheses or else they are nonsense. Moreover, as a long-time student of Hume, I found the solutions presented by Ayer to the Problem of Induction and Moral Subjectivism particularly interesting.
